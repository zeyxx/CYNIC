An **Error 404** is commonly dismissed as a standard network response code. A missing page on a remote server.

For me, it was an existential diagnosis: **Humanity Not Found**.

For years, I lived constantly on guard, unable to trust, unable to connect without calculating, unable to drop my defenses. I had reduced myself to a ghost of technical control at the expense of anything genuinely human.

This text is the autopsy of an internal collapse: how the reflex to hack the world ended up consuming my own life, and what it took to relearn the dignity of physical reality.

I grew up in an average French suburb. Average income, average house, average scenery. The textbook definition of an environment where everything looks fine on paper, yet the air is thick with a quiet, systemic decay.

In the middle of that structure sat me: the middle child (an older sister, a younger sister). Being number two in a sibling hierarchy builds a very specific survival engine: you are neither the firstborn carrying the weight of early expectations, nor the youngest being sheltered. You operate in the blind spot. You learn to observe in total silence, dissect micro-tensions, and map the emotional vulnerabilities of your surroundings to preempt chaos.

Very early on, I realized my brain was not wired like everyone else's in the room. Unmedicated, undiagnosed hardcore ASD/AuDHD. Not a neat psychiatric label to make people comfortable, but the raw, abrasive reality of it: unfiltered sensory input, inverted social parsing, and a complete inability to participate in the hypocritical theater of neurotypical standards.

The world was architected by and for neurotypicals. If you refuse their linear tracks, you are marked as a defect. At 13, the idea of mowing lawns or doing conventional side jobs for pocket money made zero sense to my dopamine circuitry. 

What stimulated my mind was the machine. Pushing Minecraft to its absolute FPS threshold, tearing down hardware, and then sinking into the competitive vortex of **Fortnite** (from Season 4 to Season 9). For an AuDHD brain, this was not just a game: it was high-speed cognitive conditioning. Endless nights shaving off milliseconds of input delay, editing walls at the speed of light, and hardwiring the ruthless logic of the Battle Royale: 100 players drop in, only one walks out. It was the primal blueprint of zero-sum PvP.

The machine did not lie. It carried no fake smiles and no implicit social double binds. It answered with pure logic.

This is the exact birthplace of the hacker archetype: **ontological solitude sublimated into the screen.**

When you feel like an alien stranded among humans, misunderstood and perpetually out of phase, the weight of isolation becomes intolerable. And when a kid has only ever learned how to survive, his ego constructs a defense: **misanthropy**.

*“I am not an outcast because I am broken. I am isolated because the herd is cowardly, hypocritical, and predictable.”*

Misanthropy was never a philosophical stance: it was ballistic body armor. Scorning the crowd shielded me from feeling the sting of their rejection. Treating human beings as deterministic variables, exploitable pawns, or targets for logic bugs allowed me to look down from above, when in reality I was merely a terrified kid hiding from his own loneliness.

## Act I: The Thesis: Masking, Predation, and Multimodal Hacking

Operating as an outlier in a normative world demands an exorbitant price: **masking**.

To avoid being pulverized by the pack, to never relive that foundational 6th-grade trauma where I was singled out and branded as "the small one," I learned to act. I engineered synthetic personas. Deception became my primary defense. I fragmented my identity into watertight silos between my physical peers and my nocturnal operations.

Yet very quickly, that survival reflex mutated into something far more insidious: **multimodal hacking**.

Hacking was no longer just late nights in a shell terminal; it had become a reflex: a compulsive drive to probe vulnerabilities in every system (human, emotional, relational) to bypass friction and extract outcomes without paying the real cost. Short-circuiting time, effort, and above all, the fear of vulnerability. Treating my body, loved ones, and society as architectures to be reverse-engineered so I would never risk being touched.

### The Underbelly: The Illusion of Omnipotence

Let’s rewind to the marrow of the story. Why does a 13-year-old kid spend his nights glued to injection tools and vulnerability scanners?

The answer is twofold: the illusion of control and the dopamine slot machine.

In the physical world, I was defenseless, an awkward and rejected kid. But behind a terminal, probing misconfigured servers and watching data streams dump onto my screen gave me the illusion of total control. It was the overcompensation of a powerless boy.

The technical reality was far from the Hollywood myth. I was never a movie hacker engineering zero-day exploits in the dark. I was a methodical, sharp script kiddie. My edge was not reinventing the wheel, but running targeted research on Google Dorks to uncover targets that public scanners missed. For extraction, I relied on proven recipes: SQLi Dumper, sqlmap, later RustyDumper or XDumpGo. I operated like a wholesaler of raw data: inject, extract massive dumps, sort the raw feed, without wasting time recoding tools when functional machinery was already available.

It all started even earlier, cheating on Minecraft to feed an inferior ego (autoclickers, lightweight injected clients for reach and knockback). Then the underground exploration slid toward petty predation. On one hand: stolen databases, run through a Minecraft credential checker, to mass-loot the Hypixel Skyblock profiles of strangers and flip their items for scraps, barely eighty euros total. On the other, the rawest betrayal: using a password pattern a friend had once trusted me with to force my way into his account and strip his entire inventory with an accomplice. When he figured it out, I denied it. We laughed. That laugh was not joy: it was the moral anesthesia of a kid incapable of owning the betrayal of a friend. Thousands of hours of grind, destroyed for nothing, out of sheer vanity. But the boomerang always returns: what you sow in contempt, you eventually pay for in full.

Above all, it was an unregulated casino. Watching data logs scroll past in real time. Hitting technical dead ends one night, then striking an unexpected vein of vulnerable configurations the next. And at the end of the wire: small crypto tips or digital trades for technically trivial work. My AuDHD brain had tasted the most potent variable reward loop on the internet: frictionless dopamine on tap, which instantly warped my relationship to the value of labor and the necessity of boundaries.

Inevitably, the technical obsession skirted the underground: gray-market forums, Discord backchannels, easy digital cash, teenage rivalries behind disposable handles. But every crossed boundary carved a quiet rot into my conscience. The constant anxiety of hearing a knock at the door at dawn, the gnawing dissonance of knowing my comfort came at the expense of everyday people, the paranoia eating away at my sleep... I had integrated myself into the very machinery of predation I despised.

### Evasion and Digital Anesthesia

While I played the formidable ghost behind a keyboard, my actual life was an unbroken sequence of evasions.

Deep down, I was starved for recognition, terrified of being an invisible defect. To capture attention without risking vulnerability, I built a cover: **the clown persona**. I loved making people laugh, which was genuine, but it also served as social camouflage. As long as I kept the audience entertained, I held the spotlight and kept rejection at bay.

The tragedy of the clown is that the audience only watches the performance; nobody sees the human behind the curtain.

Whenever someone attempted to step past the comedy and connect with who I actually was, my nervous system short-circuited. Pure panic of vulnerability. Total freeze mode. An absolute inability to stand exposed in physical reality and sustain authentic connection without armor. Every retreat left behind a growing pile of things unsaid and undone.

And to avoid confronting the shame of that recurring cowardice, my mind retreated into virtual consumption and digital anesthesia: existing by proxy behind glowing feeds to evade the friction of the real.

In 2020, the COVID-19 lockdowns demolished my remaining physical guardrails. Confined eighteen hours a day to my bedroom in front of glowing screens, stripped of friction with the tactile world, my mind began spinning in a closed loop. It was within this total isolation that my first magical thinking and delusional narratives took root: deprived of physical embodiment, an overclocked AuDHD brain compensates for powerlessness by fabricating scenarios of imaginary omnipotence. The virtual had permanently superseded physical reality.

It was also fertile ground for conspiratorial rabbit holes across the web. When you suffer from a world you cannot decipher, the easiest escape is adopting lazy narratives: finding a convenient scapegoat, blaming a religious or ethnic group for pulling the strings just to give chaos an identifiable face. It took time and intellectual maturity to see through that dead end: predation and the impulse to dominate are not the hallmark of any specific group, but structural invariants of human nature whenever a centralized system grants power without verifiable checks.

One concrete snapshot from that period: a neobank launching its service was offering €2 credited per referral. The displayed mechanism was simple: one email address, one link validation, and the counter incremented. A friend and I let naive enthusiasm take over: we generated and validated over a thousand laposte.net email addresses to stack referrals. When the campaign closed, the displayed balance transformed into "sponsored users pending verification" (national ID, supporting documents, the standard KYC protocol). That initial display had been misleading for a kid who didn't know the adult rulebook. It was my first trauma with the system: the innocent optimism of a child convinced he had found a legal loophole, erased by the fine print of terms and conditions. For once, the system had played me instead of the other way around.

### Torn Shoes and the Search for Legitimacy

By stacking fractions of Bitcoin during the bear market and monetizing these teenage technical experiments, I had built up several thousand dollars in virtual savings. It was never a static lump sum: it was a sliding river of digital sand slipping through my fingers.

Yet that virtual money burned my palms. Possessing those early sums without legitimate physical effort filled me with an deep sense of fraudulence. I couldn't hold onto it without trying to dispose of it. I bought electric scooters to anchor the value into tangible objects, then bought endless rounds of drinks at parties to buy social integration. The reclusive kid funded everyone else's night out just to earn the right to sit in the room.

I was 15 years old when the adult world imploded onto my shoulders.

Right before summer, my parents' marriage fractured violently, plunging the household into financial suffocation. Both turned to me: an overdue utility bill arrived alongside a blown clutch on my father's car. Convinced I had to play the savior to keep the roof over our heads, I handed over €1,800, my entire liquid cash reserves at the time.

I spent that entire summer without knowing if I would ever see a dime of it back. I walked the streets with a single presentable outfit and a pair of worn-out shoes with holes in the soles.

It was during this period that the poison of internal division became permanently entrenched.

To numb the financial panic and the constant fear of abandonment, I retreated into solitary, habitual cannabis use. The masking became absolute: externally, I projected the image of a capable, precocious, independent kid handling adult crises; internally, I was a terrified teenager locking himself in a cloud of smoke the second my bedroom door closed. That double life carved a quiet moral fault line inside me: functioning impeccably in my external responsibilities, yet drifting entirely away from the truth of my own being.

### The Perp Liquidation Massacre: Two Years Up in Smoke

In an attempt to redeem myself in my own eyes, I turned to crypto trading as a vehicle for redemption through merit: taking those digital savings and channeling them into genuine market analysis, converting virtual gains into legitimate capital through pure analytical skill.

The illusion was brief.

The constant fear of missing the train, being left on the platform, and losing the singular opportunity that might finally save me was an agonizing poison. It was FOMO that pushed me into irrational leverage, rushing every decision directly into self-sabotage. Night after night stoned on hash, paralyzed in front of cascading red candles, watching liquidation engines wipe out my accounts in seconds.

The next morning, with burning eyes and a stomach knotted in financial dread, I sat in advanced calculus and physics without having slept a single minute.

Over two years, every single dollar I had spent years accumulating vanished. I was liquidated more times than I can count, taking over five full cross-margin wipes between Hyperliquid and centralized exchanges.
It was not merely financial ruin: it was psychological demolition. The constant uncertainty, the terror of sudden wicks, the humiliation of having my rawest weaknesses exposed... I was brought to my knees.

The downward spiral accelerated between November 2025 and March 2026.
Minimal sleep. Heavy daily hash consumption to numb the anxiety. Feedback loops with local LLM agents, locking myself into manic architectures ungrounded from physical constraints. The academic fallout was immediate: I failed my second year of higher studies in cybersecurity, purely because I had lost the capacity to manage my baseline life.

The stark contrast between the sovereign builder glimpsed during that day and the isolation of my daily routine became unbearable. My armor cracked: the sheer exhaustion of maintaining a façade of control, the shame of having derailed my studies, and the internal void finally caught up with me.

Yet in that emotional vacuum, I tried synthetic party drugs, something I had sworn never to touch. Stripped of romance, those substances were nothing more than a destructive escape. Real perspective never came from chemicals; it came from direct conversations with real people looking me in the eye. No substance brings clarity: the only real ground lies in daily actions and honest human connection.

Since that spring, the process of waking up has been a slow, demanding discipline, full of daily highs and lows, gradually learning to look reality dead in the face.

The final emergency brake did not arrive in a cinematic flash: it hit **this month, in October 2026**.

After a final spiral where accumulated fatigue and chemical numbing pushed me to the brink, the moral nausea became absolute. The clear shock of having violated my own integrity, the physical disgust of intoxicating myself to dodge the terror of existence, the realization that my body was actively rejecting the poison: I pulled the emergency brake with both hands. **Permanent sobriety. The definitive end of artificial heavens. A return to dignity.**

The smoke cleared. I relearned how to breathe, felt the earth’s gravity under my feet, began repairing ties with my family, and stepped firmly into physical reality. I stopped hiding inside fantasies and accepted my responsibility as a man.

That is where **CYNIC** took root: not as a boy's manic fever dream running from his ghosts, but as the focused codebase of a builder facing reality.
